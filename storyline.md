# The Gin Defense of Wood Science Village — Full Storyline

The complete narrative arc, act by act, as it plays out in `index.html`. Each
trial is a joke on the surface — but each one is also secretly built around
something true about Jonas. That's noted after each trial below, in case you
want to say it out loud when you introduce that round (works well as a
toast line, or just something to keep in your back pocket while you present).

---

## Prologue — Wood Science Village

Every night, the village gathers at Jonas' Gin Emporium — botanists,
brewers, the occasional wandering bard — for gin poured with a scientist's
precision and a poet's excess.

Two faces in that crowd get a proper introduction: **Jonas**, the Gin
Alchemist, who has spent years perfecting the old ways of the still
("Botanicals are my weapons"); and **Druidingo**, keeper of the wild
botanicals, sworn to see the craft endure ("More botanicals! More
friends!").

Across the square stands **The Rusty Mug** — once the loudest tavern in
the village, its tables sitting a little emptier each night as the crowds
drift toward the Emporium's warm lights. Its owner, **Falkorc**, Tavern
Legend, famed for his cocktail nights ("Good drinks bring people
together!"), has watched gin quietly win over his public for long enough.

Enough watching. Falkorc marches into the Emporium and slams a challenge
on the bar: one battle, fair and public, for the thirsty inhabitants of
Wood Science Village. Whoever makes the better gin keeps **both** bars.

Three trials will decide it.

---

## Trial I — The Protocol Trials

Before a gin may be called a gin, its making must be written so that any
hand could repeat it. Each contender enters their craft into the record —
opens OpenBIS, logs the three ingredients (Grain Spirit, Orange Peel,
Coriander Seeds) into "The Gin Making Space," connects their protocol to
the objects they created, and gives the whole thing a fancy name.

Contenders go in order of seniority: **Druidingo**, then **Falkorc**, then
**Jonas**. Only two carry the trial forward — the Ledger speaks, the
finalists are named, and a grade is set for each protocol actually
submitted.

**What this really honors:** Jonas has amazing protocols, and genuinely
loves registering them in OpenBIS. The in-game "Protocol Trials" aren't
really a joke about bureaucracy — they're a straight-faced tribute to
someone who documents his work properly because he wants to, not because
he has to.

---

## Trial II — Naming the Gin

Everyone in the village knows a gin's tasting name carries more than
flavor — it carries feeling, memory, longing, the whole unreasonable
weight of a good evening. And there's no truer test of a gin than the
tonic poured beside it.

Each contender mixes three gin tonics — pick the ingredient, raise the
bottle, tilt it to the line, land as close to the target as the widgets
allow. Jonas's bottle behaves. Falkorc's, for reasons nobody can explain,
pours a great deal more eagerly than it should — the response curve keeps
drifting mid-pour, and even a raised bottle won't hold still in his hand.

Jonas's three pours become, whatever the grade: *Lemon peel remembering a
summer it never had* · *Post-rain rosemary with emotionally distant
grapefruit* · *Pine forest nostalgia with unresolved coriander tension*.
Falkorc's become: *Mint* · *Coriander* · *Gin*.

**What this really honors:** Jonas has a high technological bar for
himself — he builds (and appreciates) genuinely well-engineered tools.
The naming widget is the most elaborate, fussiest, most over-built
mechanic in the whole show on purpose: a real bottle that visibly lifts
and tilts, a liquid level masked pixel-for-pixel to the actual glass
artwork, three different pour ratios across three rounds. That's the
joke and the tribute in one move — of course *his* gin gets the fancy
widget.

---

## Trial III — The Puzzle of Tastes

By now the village already leans toward Jonas's gin — you can hear it in
the square. But Falkorc gets one last chance: a crazy slogan for his gin,
matched against Jonas's own, judged against a great puzzle cube whose
faces bear not colors but the sigils of science itself. One minute on the
clock. Whichever slogans the cube actually matches, for either house, are
the ones that stick.

The night's full ledger goes up for the village to see — protocol grade,
the three flavors each contender mixed, the slogans each one landed — and
then the crowd decides who has the best gin. If they favor Falkorc for a
moment, it doesn't last: a louder chant rises from the back of the square
and doesn't stop. *"Jonas! Jonas! Jonas!"*

**What this really honors:** Jonas has spent his PhD dealing with many
sample orientations and complex moisture relations — genuinely difficult,
multi-variable puzzles, solved one careful match at a time. He's basically
a puzzle master already. Handing him an actual physical puzzle cube for
the final trial isn't a random party game — it's the one challenge that's
closest to what he already does for a living, dressed up as a drinking
game.

---

## Epilogue

By nightfall the whole square is toasting — Ingo, Jonas, even Falkorc
himself, glass raised beside the owlbear statue. Two bars, one crowd, one
very loud verdict.

And underneath all of it, the one line that's true regardless of how any
individual round happened to go:

> **In every world that matters: Jonas Maas has successfully defended his
> thesis.**
